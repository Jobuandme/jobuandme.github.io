BF6 UI Redesign

My Objective

Battlefield 6 launched to a great reception, but the original UI didn't land the same way. Players called it cluttered and hard to navigate. So I redesigned it myself as a fan project.

I pulled feedback from Reddit, Microsoft Store reviews, and my own playtesting, then focused on four screens: Home Screen, Multiplayer, Server Browser, Campaign

My Goals

Address player feedback, smoothen the user journey, and add missing features.

What Actual Players Were Saying

Before designing anything, I needed to know exactly what was causing players problems. Reddit gave me the clearest, most honest read on that. Here's what kept coming up:

  • The horizontal "Netflix-style" scrolling felt overused and made navigation confusing.
  • The featured area on the home screen was mostly ads.
  • Too much information competed for attention - popular modes were hard to find.
  • There was no real server browser - no way to choose a specific server.

This told me exactly what to fix: reduce the information overload, improve navigation, and bring back some of that classic Battlefield style.

Once I had a first prototype, I posted it to Reddit for feedback. There was a ton of support, but there was also a clear consensus that the multiplayer screen wasn't there yet. That feedback shaped the next drafts.

See what the Reddit community had to say about my first revision here: www.reddit.com/r/my_bf6_ui_redesign

The Home Screen

My Updated Home Screen Vs The Original:

The Problem

The original screen had a cramped layout, ad space taking up the featured area, and horizontal scrolling that buried important options.

The Fix:

  • Removed ads entirely, freeing up the space for important content and buttons.
  • Split Multiplayer and Campaign into their own screens instead of cramming both into one.
  • Made Multiplayer, Campaign, Portal, and Training bold and high-contrast, so the primary actions are impossible to miss.
  • Leaned on progressive disclosure - showing information step by step instead of all at once - to keep each screen focused.

My Thinking:

I took inspiration from Battlefield 3 and 4, since their layouts were simple and well-liked. Splitting the Campaign and Multiplayer screens adds an extra step to the user journey - but it trades one overwhelming screen for two simple, sequential ones.

(Worth noting: the original team had ad space and a store in their requirements. I didn't have those constraints, so I excluded them for the sake of this redesign.)

The Multiplayer Screen

My multiplayer screens went through the most revisions compared to the rest of the designs. The feedback I gathered from real people on Reddit heavily influenced and improved the final designs for these screens.

My first design used large tiles. Players pointed out two key things I'd missed:

  • Most players are on 16"+ screens, so there was room for more information and smaller text than I'd assumed.
  • Big tiles wasted space that could show more modes at once.

The final design

  • Switched large tiles for a compact, scannable layout - players can see every mode without scrolling through a long list.
  • Visually separated the Server Browser from the rest of the modes, since it isn't a traditional mode like the others.

The Server Browser

This mode didn't exist in the original UI at all - players had no way to pick a specific server.

My Thinking

  • Server data sits in a structured bar, stacked into a clean vertical list with a clear hierarchy and little clutter.
  • Small thumbnails act as visual anchors, so players can recognize a familiar map or server at a glance without reading any text.
  • The bottom buttons use a classic Battlefield style, paired with clear icons, so returning players instantly know what does what.

The whole idea of this design was to get players into a server fast, without menu friction taking from their play time.

The Campaign Screen

My Thinking

The original UI never had a dedicated campaign screen, so I had free reign for the design of this screen.

I intentionally used minimal on-screen text to keep a focus on a few missions at a time, rather than the whole list. I also kept the buttons at the bottom of the screen subtle to still be able to guide navigation without distracting from the main content.
My goal was to make the campaign feel like a cinematic experience, rather than a menu.

Final Thoughts

This was my favorite UI/UX project to date, and working on a game I'm a long-time fan of made it even better. The Reddit feedback shaped lots of major decisions here, and seeing the community respond to the final result was a huge confidence boost.

I intentionally left out features like the store and ad space, leaving them out made me appreciate just how much bigger a full-scale UI project really is. I also never reallydisliked Battlefield 6's original menus, this was more of a fan project that allowed me to work in a setting im passionate about. I learned a lot about how to design for a specific audience, and how to take feedback from real users and turn it into real design decisions.
